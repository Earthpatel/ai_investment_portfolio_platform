Why does switching win about 2/3 of the time?

Your first pick hits the car with probability 1/3, and nothing the host does changes that. The host knows where the car is and always opens a goat door you didn’t pick. So the 2/3 chance that the car was “somewhere else” collapses onto the single remaining closed door. Staying wins only if your first guess was right (1/3); switching wins whenever it was wrong (2/3).

This is Monte Carlo in a nutshell: when the logic feels slippery, simulate the experiment many times and count. The more trials, the closer the estimate gets to the truth: the error shrinks roughly like 1/√N. The same idea powers the investment simulator: instead of doors, each “trial” is a possible future for a portfolio.
